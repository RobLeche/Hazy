# Risk communication

- Headline pattern: **[hazard] [likelihood] [when]**, e.g. "Storms likely this afternoon",
  "Flooding possible on low roads tonight".
- Always pair a label with a text level and a reason; never colour alone.
- Distinguish **NWS alerts** (official, shown first, labelled "National Weather Service")
  from **Hazy outlook** (labelled "Hazy outlook, not an official warning").
- Express uncertainty honestly: "possible", "likely"; no false precision (no "73% flood chance").
- Safety actions only from authoritative phrasing: "Turn Around, Don't Drown" for flooded roads;
  "When thunder roars, go indoors" for lightning.
- Show the update time and the location's timezone.
- Avoid alarmist words ("catastrophic") unless quoting an NWS alert.
- Footer on risk screens: "Hazy is not a warning service. For official alerts see weather.gov."
